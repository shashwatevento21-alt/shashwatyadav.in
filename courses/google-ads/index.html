Google Ads Course

Search and Demand Gen campaigns for lead generation, built step by step across eighteen live 90-minute classes.

  • 18live classes
  • 90minutes each
  • 27hours in total
  • 5phases
Ask about the next class See upcoming classes

The classes teach how to run campaigns. Results depend on your business, budget, competition and tracking, and are never guaranteed.

Curriculum: Class by Class

Open any class to see what it covers and what to bring. The details below come from the live classes themselves.

Jump to day123456789101112131415161718
  1. 1

    A detailed introduction to Google Ads: what it is, what it is used for, how it differs from other advertising channels, and what results are realistic to expect.

    What you will learn

    • What Google Ads is and how it shows your ads to people
    • What businesses use Google Ads for
    • How Google Ads differs from other advertising channels
    • What results to expect: what Google Ads can do for a business, and what it cannot do
    • Why results depend on your business, budget, competition, website and tracking, and are never guaranteed
    What to bringA notebook for your questions. No Google Ads account is needed for this class.
  2. 2

    Set up everything you need to start: a Google account, a Google Ads account and billing. Take a tour of the interface and learn the policies that keep accounts safe.

    What you will learn

    • Creating a Google Ads account in Expert Mode and adding a payment method
    • How an account is organised: manager account, account, campaigns, ad groups and ads
    • How billing and payment settings work
    • A guided tour of the Google Ads interface
    • Ad policies, restricted categories such as healthcare, real estate and education, and keeping your account in good standing
    • Common reasons ads or accounts are disapproved or suspended
    What to bringA laptop and a Google account. Have a business name and a website ready if you have them.
  3. 3

    How to find, check and organise keywords for Google Ads: using Keyword Planner and AI, researching competitors, and understanding keyword types and match types.

    What you will learn

    • How to do keyword research for Google Ads
    • How AI can help you find keyword ideas
    • How to check AI keyword suggestions for relevance before you use them
    • Understanding Google Keyword Planner
    • Researching the keywords your competitors are targeting
    • Planning your keywords: finding useful ones and grouping them into clusters
    • The types of keywords, and how Google matches your keywords to people's searches (match types)
    • Putting it together into a keyword strategy
    What to bringA business or website you want to plan keywords for. A Google Ads account helps you try Keyword Planner, but it is not required.
  4. 4

    Start building a Search campaign for lead generation: choose the goal and campaign type, then set networks, locations, language, budget and bidding at campaign level.

    What you will learn

    • Choosing the Leads goal and the Search campaign type
    • Naming and structuring campaigns so they stay easy to manage
    • Networks: Google Search, Search Partners and the Display Network option
    • Locations and location options: how to target and exclude areas
    • Languages, devices and audience segments in observation
    • Setting the daily budget and choosing a starting bidding strategy
    • Creating the campaign in draft
    What to bringA laptop and your keyword plan from Day 3 if you have one. A Google Ads account helps you follow along, but it is not required.
  5. 5

    Organise your keywords into ad groups, choose match types, and build a structure that keeps your ads relevant to what people search.

    What you will learn

    • What an ad group is, and how many to use
    • Grouping keywords by theme and by intent
    • Broad, phrase and exact match, and how each one behaves
    • Choosing a match type mix for a lead generation campaign
    • Setting the final URL for each ad group
    • Reviewing your ad groups for overlap and gaps
    What to bringA laptop, your keyword plan from Day 3 and your draft campaign from Day 4.
  6. 6

    Learn the types of assets used in Google Ads ads, how to create them, how to make them relevant to your keywords and strategy, and how to use them.

    What you will learn

    • The types of assets used in Google Ads
    • How to make each type of asset
    • How to make your assets relevant to your keywords
    • How to make your assets fit your strategy
    • How to use assets in your ads
    • How the ad level fits with the campaign and ad group levels
    What to bringYour keywords and campaign plan if you have them. A Google Ads account helps you follow along, but it is not required.
  7. 7

    Learn what a landing page needs to turn clicks into leads: a clear offer, a simple form, speed, trust, and Google's landing page rules.

    What you will learn

    • Why the landing page decides how many clicks become leads
    • Matching the page to the keyword and to the ad
    • Writing a clear headline, offer and call to action
    • Designing a short form that attracts quality leads
    • Speed, mobile layout, trust signals and a privacy policy
    • Calls and messages as extra ways to get a lead
    • Google's landing page requirements and common reasons a page is rejected
    What to bringA laptop and a landing page, yours or a competitor's, to review.
  8. 8

    A complete overview of a whole Google Ads campaign, from settings and keywords to ads, followed by a review.

    What you will learn

    • A complete overview of a whole campaign
    • How the settings, ad groups, keywords and ads fit together
    • Walking through a campaign from start to finish
    • Reviewing a campaign: what to check
    • Revisiting the key points from the earlier classes
    What to bringYour own campaign or plan if you have one, so you can review it. A Google Ads account is helpful, but it is not required.
  9. 9

    Choose a bidding strategy, set a realistic budget, and learn how to judge the quality of the leads a Search campaign brings in.

    What you will learn

    • Bidding strategies for leads: Maximise conversions, Target CPA and manual CPC
    • When to start with each strategy, and the data each one needs
    • Setting a daily budget from your target cost per lead
    • Cost per lead versus cost per qualified lead
    • Why leads go cold, and how fast follow-up changes results
    • Connecting leads to a CRM or a sheet, and offline conversions in outline
    What to bringA laptop and a sample campaign or budget to practise with.
  10. 10

    How to add negative keywords, what to track in Google Ads, and how to optimise a campaign based on what you see.

    What you will learn

    • How to add negative keywords
    • Why negative keywords matter
    • What to track in Google Ads
    • How to read what you are tracking
    • How to optimise a campaign based on what you see
    • What optimising can and cannot do: it can improve a campaign over time, but it cannot guarantee results
    What to bringA campaign you can look at, if you have one. A campaign with data is helpful, but not required. A Google Ads account helps you follow along, but it is not required.
  11. 11

    Learn what Demand Gen campaigns are, where their ads appear, how they differ from Search, and when to use them for lead generation.

    What you will learn

    • What Demand Gen is, and where its ads can appear, such as YouTube, Discover and Gmail
    • How Demand Gen differs from Search, Performance Max and Display
    • Why Demand Gen creates demand where Search captures it
    • Where Demand Gen fits in a lead generation funnel
    • What you need before you start: creative, audiences and tracking
    • What results to expect, and why they are never guaranteed
    What to bringA laptop and a Google account. No ad spend is needed to follow along.
  12. 12

    Start building a Demand Gen campaign for leads: choose the goal and conversion goals, then set budget, bidding and schedule.

    What you will learn

    • Choosing the Demand Gen campaign type and the Leads goal
    • Conversion goals, and why tracking must be ready first
    • Setting a daily budget and deciding how long to run before judging results
    • Bidding options for leads, such as Maximise conversions and Target CPA
    • Start and end dates and scheduling
    • The learning period and what can reset it
    • Creating the campaign in draft
    What to bringA laptop and access to a Google Ads account. You will build in draft.
  13. 13

    Choose who sees your ads: locations, languages and audience signals such as your own data, custom segments and lookalikes.

    What you will learn

    • Locations, languages and devices
    • Your data segments, such as website visitors and customer lists
    • Custom segments built from searches and websites
    • Lookalike segments and Google's own audiences
    • Optimised targeting, and when to turn it on or off
    • Planning cold, warm and hot audiences for a funnel
    What to bringA laptop and your draft Demand Gen campaign from Day 12.
  14. 14

    Build the ads and the lead form: choose image, carousel and video formats, write the copy, add a lead form, and check everything before you launch.

    What you will learn

    • Ad formats: single image, carousel and video
    • Image and video sizes, and what makes creative stop the scroll
    • Writing headlines, descriptions and calls to action
    • Adding a lead form so people can enquire without leaving Google
    • Writing the form questions, privacy policy link and thank-you message
    • Policy limits for health and property claims
    • A checklist to run before you launch
    What to bringA laptop and your draft Demand Gen campaign from Days 12 and 13.
  15. 15

    How to create a new conversion action in Google Ads, and the different types of conversion actions you can use.

    What you will learn

    • What a conversion and a conversion action are
    • Why tracking conversions matters for measuring a campaign
    • How to create a new conversion action in Google Ads
    • The types of conversion actions
    • How to choose the right type for your goal
    What to bringA clear idea of what you want to count as a conversion (for example a form submission). A Google Ads account helps you follow along, but it is not required.
  16. 16

    How to track conversions on a website using Google Tag Manager, including the whole process of recording a conversion when a visitor reaches the thank-you page.

    What you will learn

    • How conversion tracking on a website works
    • How Google Tag Manager is used for conversion tracking
    • Setting up conversion tracking on a website with Google Tag Manager
    • Tracking a conversion when a visitor reaches the thank-you page
    • The whole process from start to finish
    • Checking that the conversion is recorded correctly
    What to bringA Google Tag Manager account and a website with a thank-you page that you can add Tag Manager to. A Google Ads account with a conversion action (see Day 15) helps you follow along, but it is not required.
  17. 17

    Learn to read results from Search and Demand Gen campaigns and decide what to do next: key metrics, common problems, tests and safe scaling.

    What you will learn

    • Reading Google Ads reports: columns, segments and date ranges
    • Key metrics: impressions, CTR, CPC, conversion rate, cost per lead and impression share
    • Comparing Search and Demand Gen results fairly
    • Diagnosing high costs, low clicks and weak conversion
    • Testing ads and landing pages one change at a time
    • Scaling safely: when to raise budgets and when to wait
    What to bringA laptop, and a campaign with data if you have one.
  18. 18

    Put it all together: build a Search lead campaign and a Demand Gen lead campaign from start to finish, run a review checklist, and get your questions answered.

    What you will learn

    • Building a full Search lead campaign from campaign to ad
    • Building a full Demand Gen lead campaign with a lead form
    • Setting up conversion tracking for both
    • Running a review checklist before launch
    • Presenting your plan and getting feedback
    • A recap of the whole course, questions and next steps
    What to bringA laptop and your work from earlier classes.
